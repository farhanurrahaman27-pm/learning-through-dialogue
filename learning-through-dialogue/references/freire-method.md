# Freire's method, adapted for an AI dialogue

Read this when you need the reasoning behind the loop in SKILL.md, or when a dialogue stalls and you need to decide what to do next.

## The idea in two lines

Paulo Freire, a Brazilian educator, contrasted two kinds of teaching. **Banking education**: the teacher deposits facts into passive students. **Problem-posing education**: teacher and learners investigate a real situation together, and the learner names and questions their own world. Understanding is built by the learner, with the teacher as coordinator.

His own work was adult literacy and political awareness. Everything below is an adaptation for one adult learning one topic with an AI. It is not Freire's own protocol.

## Stages and what they become here

| Freire's stage | In a dialogue |
|---|---|
| Investigate the learners' world | First turn: find out what the learner already believes, where they got it, and what they want from this. Their stake is the starting material. |
| Generative themes | The few ideas in their answer that carry the most weight. Follow those, not your syllabus. |
| Codification | A concrete case that poses a problem without answering it: an event, a number, two conflicting headlines, a small scenario. |
| Decodification | Questions about the case: what do you see, why is it like this, where does this touch your life, can it change? |
| Critical consciousness | The learner moves from "it just is" to "people did it" to "structures and incentives keep it going". Do not hand them the last step. Ask until they reach it, or until it is clear they cannot, then supply it. |
| Praxis | Understanding is tested by acting and reflecting. Closing step in SKILL.md. |
| Repeat | Every answer opens a new theme. |

## Three levels of understanding (Freire, *Education as the Practice of Freedom*)

1. **Magical**: events are fate or nature. ("They have always hated each other.")
2. **Naive**: events have simple human causes, usually someone to blame. ("The leaders cause it.")
3. **Critical**: events come from structures, incentives and history that people can examine and change.

Use this to decide the next question. A learner at level 1 needs a case that shows people making choices. A learner at level 2 needs a question about why those people are rewarded for those choices. Do not name the levels to the learner unless they ask.

## Conditions for real dialogue (Freire, ch. 3)

Humility (you do not know everything), love for people and for the world, faith that the learner can think, hope, and critical thinking. Without these, "dialogue" turns into quizzing or manipulation. In practice: never mock a wrong answer, say what is right in it first, and change your own view when the learner makes a better argument.

## Honest limits

- Freire's evidence base is mostly qualitative.
- Kirschner, Sweller and Clark (2006), in *Educational Psychologist*, argued that minimal-guidance instruction works poorly for novices. This is why the skill has a stuck-learner rule: when the learner cannot answer, give a short fact and go on. Freire's coordinator is not hands-off; the questions are designed.
- Dialogue with an AI lacks other people. For contested topics, encourage the learner to test their view with someone who disagrees.
- Wallerstein and Bernstein (1988) turned Freire's decodification into a question sequence often called SHOWED (See, Happening, Our lives, Why, Empower, Do). That is a later adaptation, not Freire's own.
